Late fee versus interest on a late invoice

A late fee you invent after the work is done usually does not stick

If the contract, the quote, or the purchase order never mentioned a late fee, putting "$50 late fee" on a reminder is a new demand. Many clients will ignore it. Some will pay the original amount and dispute the extra. A judge or an arbitrator, if it ever got that far, would look at what was agreed before the work, not at what you added once you were unpaid.

Write the rule in the terms the client accepts before you start. Then print the same rule on every invoice so accounts payable sees it without opening a second file. If you change the rule, change it for the next job, in writing, not for an invoice that is already outstanding.

A late fee is a flat amount or a percentage of that invoice, charged once when the invoice is late. Example language, not a recommendation: "If payment arrives more than 7 days after the due date, a late fee of X applies." Whether X is allowed where you and the client are located is a local-law question. This page does not pick a number and does not say that any particular percentage is "normal" in a way you can copy blindly.

Interest and a flat late fee are different tools

Interest is a rate that keeps running while the invoice is unpaid. A flat late fee is one extra amount. They answer different problems. Interest is for long delays. A flat fee is for the cost of chasing a single missed cycle. Stacking both without clear language is how invoices look punitive.

If you use interest, name the rate, the compounding (or that it does not compound), and the start date (the day after due, or after a grace period). "Interest may apply" is not a rate. "1.5% per month" is a rate, but whether that rate is allowed depends on the governing law of the contract. Some places cap commercial interest. Some treat a very high rate as a penalty and refuse it. Look up the rule for the place named in your contract. Do not copy a percentage from a forum post.

Consumer credit and commercial invoices are not the same pile of law. A freelance invoice to a company is usually a commercial contract. A bill to a private individual may trigger consumer rules. If you do not know which you are sending, that is the first thing to fix, before the fee clause.

This page does not recommend a rate, a fee, or a product. It explains the difference so you can write a sentence that means one thing.

State and country rules vary — this page does not pick one

The United States leaves a lot of this to state law and to the contract. The United Kingdom and other countries have their own late-payment regimes for commercial debts, including statutory interest in some cases. Those regimes have conditions. They are not a sticker you put on any PDF.

If your client is in another country, you have at least two legal systems in play. The contract should say which law governs. If it does not, you will spend more on the argument than on the invoice.

Do not cite a statute you have not opened. Do not tell a client "the law says you owe 8 percent" unless you have the named law in front of you and it applies to that invoice. Wrong legal citations in a reminder make the rest of the reminder weaker.

Write the rule before you send the first invoice

A usable clause, in plain language, answers four questions:

  1. When is the invoice late (the due date, plus any grace days)
  2. What extra amount applies (flat fee, interest, or one of them)
  3. What you will pause (new work, files, license) if the contract allows
  4. How the client can ask a question without the fee clock being a surprise

Put the same four answers on the invoice footer. Keep the numbers identical to the contract. If the contract says 7 days' grace and the invoice says none, you have two documents.

Collecting a fee is a separate job from stating one. Many freelancers never collect the fee and still want the clause because it makes the due date real. That is a business choice. A clause you will never enforce is still better than a fee you invent in week six.

If the amount is large, or the client is a public body, get the wording checked by a person qualified in that jurisdiction. This article will not do that job.

FAQ

Can I add a late fee after the invoice is already overdue

You can ask. They can say no. A fee that was not in the agreement is a new offer, not an automatic extra. Put the rule in the next contract if you want it next time. For the current invoice, reminders and pausing new work (if allowed) are the tools you actually have.

Is 1.5 percent per month a normal rate

It appears in many US commercial templates. "Appears in templates" is not the same as "allowed where you are" and not the same as "you should use it." Some jurisdictions cap rates or treat a high rate as a penalty. This page does not adopt 1.5 percent or any other figure.

Do I need a lawyer to charge a late fee

You need an agreed clause and a local rule that does not forbid it. For a small invoice, a short plain-language clause in terms the client accepted is what most freelancers use. For a large invoice, or a public-sector client, have the clause read by someone qualified in that place. This page is not that reading.
