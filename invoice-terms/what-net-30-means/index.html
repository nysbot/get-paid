What net 30 means on an invoice

Net 30 counts 30 calendar days from the invoice date

Net 30 means the buyer is supposed to pay by the end of the 30th calendar day after the date printed on the invoice. If the invoice is dated 1 March, payment is due 31 March. If that day is a weekend or a public holiday, commercial practice often slides to the next business day, but only if the contract or the invoice says so. The phrase itself does not mention weekends.

The clock starts on the invoice date, not the day the client opens the email, and not the day they approve the work. If you send the invoice three days late, you have already given those three days away. If a client asks you to change the invoice date to a later day, they are asking you to extend the due date. That is a negotiation, not a net-30 feature.

Net 15, net 45, and net 60 work the same way with a different number of days. There is no special meaning hidden in the word "net" here. In trade credit it just means "the balance is due in this many days." It is not a discount. A 2/10 net 30 term is a different sentence: 2 percent off if paid in 10 days, otherwise the full amount in 30. If you did not offer that discount, do not print it.

Net 30 is a contract term, not a statute

No general statute in the United States or the United Kingdom forces a freelancer to offer net 30. It became common because larger buyers used it in purchase orders and smaller sellers copied the language. You can invoice due on receipt. You can invoice net 7. You can invoice 50 percent up front. What you can enforce later is what the client agreed to before the work, or what they accepted by paying a previous invoice on those terms.

If a purchase order says net 60 and your invoice says net 30, the purchase order often wins inside that company. Read the order before you start. If you cannot live with net 60, say so before you work, not on the day you send the PDF.

Public bodies and some large companies have their own payment-period rules. Those rules bind them, not you, unless you signed them. The UK Prompt Payment Code and similar pledges are voluntary. They are not a substitute for a due date on your invoice.

This page is general information about invoice language. It is not legal advice and it is not a recommendation to pick one term over another.

Due on receipt is not the same as net 0

"Due on receipt" means payable when the client receives the invoice. It does not name a calendar day, so arguments start: received the email, received the post, the AP clerk saw it, the manager approved it. If you want a date, print a date. "Due 14 September 2026" is harder to debate than "due on receipt."

"Net 0" is rare and unclear. Do not use it. If you want same-day payment, write "due on the invoice date" and put that date in the due-date field.

Some clients will still pay on their internal cycle — often the next weekly or monthly payment run after approval. A due date does not move money by itself. It gives you a line to point at when you send a reminder. Without a date, the reminder has nothing to quote.

Write the due date so nobody has to decode it

Put four things on every invoice, in plain text, not only in a logo:

  1. Invoice date
  2. Due date as a calendar day
  3. Amount due, with currency
  4. How to pay (account, link, or the method you actually use)

If you also like the words "net 30," put them in a footnote. Do not make the reader count. People who process invoices at volume scan for a date field. A phrase they have to interpret gets skipped.

Send the invoice the day the work is accepted, or on the milestone date in the contract. A week of delay is a week of extra credit you did not mean to give. If the contract says you invoice monthly, invoice on a fixed day so both sides can put it in a calendar.

Keep the same terms from quote to invoice. If the quote was net 14 and the invoice is net 30, you changed the deal. If you need to change terms, say so in writing before the next job, not in the PDF for the last one.

FAQ

Does net 30 start from the day I send the invoice or the day they receive it

From the invoice date printed on the document, unless the contract says otherwise. "Send date" and "invoice date" should be the same day. If you backdate or forward-date, you are changing the due date. Receipt of email is a poor clock because nobody can prove when a message was opened.

Can a client pay after 30 days and still be on time

Not under a net-30 term. Payment on day 31 is late. Whether you charge a fee for that, pause work, or send a reminder is a separate decision, and it has to be in the agreement you already have. Being late and being in breach of a fee clause are not the same thing.

Is net 30 required by law

No general law requires a freelancer to use net 30. Some buyers require it in their purchase orders. Some governments impose maximum payment periods on themselves. Your invoice can use a shorter period if the client agreed. If they did not agree, a shorter period on the PDF will not create one.
