Most unpaid invoices are forgotten, not refused

The person who approved your work is often not the person who pays it. The invoice sits in a queue, a shared inbox, or a monthly payment run. A reminder that arrives before anyone is embarrassed usually gets a date in a calendar. A reminder that arrives after you have been waiting in silence for six weeks arrives as a conflict.

This is why a schedule exists. You decide the dates once, when you are calm. The sequence runs whether you feel like chasing that week or not. If you only chase when cash is tight, you chase at the worst moment and you sound like it.

A reminder is not a threat. It restates the invoice number, the amount, the due date, and how to pay. If a late-fee clause exists in the contract, you may point to it after the due date. If it does not exist, do not invent one in the reminder. That reads as a new demand.

A reminder before the due date is cheaper than three after

The useful reminder is the one that lands while the invoice is still on time. Three days before the due date is enough time for accounts payable to put it in the next run, and early enough that nobody has to apologize yet.

A simple sequence that does not require a new decision each time:

StepWhenTone
13 days before dueShort. Invoice number, amount, due date, pay link or account
21 day after dueSame facts. Ask if anything is blocking payment
37 days after dueFirmer. Restate terms. Ask for a date they will pay
414 days after dueFirmer still. Pause new work if the contract allows
530 days after dueFinal. Say what you will do next, and only say what you will actually do

Do not send all five as a bundle if your mailer was down. If several dates have passed, send only the latest step. A client who receives "due in three days" and "30 days overdue" on the same morning will not pay faster. They will tag you as noise.

This schedule is an operating choice, not a law. Some industries pay on a monthly cycle no matter what you send. The schedule still gives you a record of when you asked.

One message at a time, never a pile

CC the original invoice. Do not reattach five versions. Use the same subject line plus the invoice number so their thread stays in one place. If they asked you to use a purchase-order number, put that number in the subject as well. Invoices without a PO are a common reason AP will not release payment. A reminder that still lacks the PO will not fix that.

Ask one question if the invoice is late: is anything blocking payment. Then wait. A list of seven questions is a way not to get an answer.

If someone replies "we pay on the last Friday of the month," write that date down and stop the sequence until that date has passed. Continuing to ping during an agreed wait is how reminders become ignored.

Phone and chat can work for a single overdue invoice with a person you already know. They do not replace the email record. If you call, send a one-line email after: "As discussed, you'll release INV-104 on Friday." That line is what you look at 30 days later.

Stop the sequence the moment payment lands

The damage from a reminder is not the reminder. It is the reminder that arrives after they have paid. Automated sequences that do not watch the bank or the payment provider will do this. When payment is recorded, every later step must cancel the same day.

If you cannot see payments automatically, check before each send. A two-minute check is cheaper than an apology.

Do not threaten legal action you will not take. Do not threaten to publish their name. Do not threaten to contact their other vendors. Say only the next step you will actually take: pause new work, apply a late-fee clause that already exists, or send a formal demand through the channel your contract names.

This page describes an operating sequence. It is not a collection-agency script and it is not legal advice.

FAQ

How many reminders is too many

Five steps over a month is enough for most freelance invoices. Daily reminders are too many. If there is no reply after the 30-day note, repeating the same email will not create a reply. That is the point where you follow the contract (pause work, formal demand) or you write the amount off. More copies of the same paragraph are not a sixth strategy.

Should I call instead of emailing

Call if you have a named person and a number they actually answer. Still send the email so there is a timestamped record. For a company with an AP portal, the portal and the PO number matter more than a phone call to the manager who already approved the work.

What if the client never replies

Silence after a due date is information. Check that the invoice reached a real address, that the PO is on it, and that the amount matches the order. If those are correct and the 14-day and 30-day notes also got no reply, treat it as a collections problem, not a writing problem. Whether you pause work depends on the contract you already have, not on a new sentence in a reminder.

  • What net 30 means on an invoice

    Net 30 means payment is due 30 calendar days after the invoice date. It is a contract term, not a law. Print the due date.

  • Late fee versus interest on a late invoice

    A late fee is a flat amount. Interest is a rate over time. Both must be in the agreement before the invoice is late.
